# Pancreas 1. Taiwan epidemiology, risk factors, surveillance, diagnosis, molecular testing, staging and resectability: fact-check report

Fact-check date: 2026-10-29. Prepared for the Taiwanese oncology NP (專科護理師) handbook, pancreatic cancer chapter (ward practice and 專師甄審).

Scope: pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC, 胰管腺癌), with a short contrast against pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasms (pNEN, 胰臟神經內分泌腫瘤).

Confidence tags:
- **[VERIFIED]**: confirmed in a primary or official source that I opened (journal abstract or full text, official government or agency page, official dataset, official NCCN page or NCCN patient guideline).
- **[PARTIAL]**: confirmed only in a secondary source, or only part of the claim could be confirmed.
- **[UNVERIFIED]**: could not be confirmed. Do not print without checking.

Values I calculated myself are marked "(computed)". Source tags inline: [A] = PubMed abstract; [FT] = PMC full text; [DS] = official dataset I downloaded and parsed.

Method: PubMed records were pulled through NCBI E-utilities (same database as the PubMed MCP tools). Full texts came from PMC. Taiwan data came from files downloaded directly from twcr.tw and mohw.gov.tw. NCCN versions came from nccn.org category pages. The NCCN professional PDF and JNCCN full text could not be opened (login, HTTP 403), so NCCN wording comes from the **NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Pancreatic Cancer 2026** (an NCCN primary document, based on professional Version 2.2026) plus peer-reviewed reproductions of the NCCN tables.

## 0. Corrections and flags for the editor (read first)

1. **Pancreatic cancer is NOT in Taiwan's top-10 cancers by new cases, but it IS a top-10 cancer killer.** In 2023 it had 3,427 new cases, which ranks it **11th by count (computed)**. In 2025 it caused 3,204 deaths and ranked **7th among cancer deaths (5th in women)**. Deaths in one year are about 93% of new cases (computed; different years). This is the core teaching point.
2. **Taiwan's registry count for "胰臟癌" includes neuroendocrine neoplasms.** In the 2023 long-form database, adenocarcinoma was 73.45% of invasive cases. Neuroendocrine tumour (NET) was 7.53% and neuroendocrine carcinoma (NEC) was 0.82%. Do not equate "胰臟癌" registry numbers with PDAC.
3. **"KRAS ~90%" is the upper end.** Large real-world series give 83% to 89%: 88% in Singhi 2019 (n = 3,594), 89.3% in Philip 2022, 83.1% in Norton 2025 (metastatic, US) and 86% in Lena 2025. **G12C is only 1.6%** of KRAS-mutant PDAC (Norton 2025).
4. **AJCC staging edition.** Exocrine pancreas is **still AJCC 8th edition** on the AJCC "Current Staging System 2026" chart. Pancreatic NET has moved to **Version 9 (2024)**. Do not write "AJCC 9th edition" for PDAC.
5. **Typos in published reviews.** Chu & Fishman 2024 print "T1 <2 cm" and "N2 greater than 4 lymph nodes". The correct AJCC 8th definitions are **T1 ≤2 cm** and **N2 ≥4 nodes**. Corallo 2025 prints NCCN resectable venous contact as "<180°". The NCCN wording, reproduced by Chu 2024, is **≤180°** without vein contour irregularity.
6. **NCCN Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma current version is 4.2026** on nccn.org (read 2026-10-29). JNCCN published a "Version 4.2026" guideline article in October 2026 (Ko et al., *JNCCN 24*(10), e260046). Its full text was blocked (HTTP 403). Only the abstract was read.
7. **Possible NCCN change in body/tail borderline criteria.** Søreide & Jamieson (2026) still list CA contact >180° *without* aortic involvement and with an intact GDA (modified Appleby procedure possible) as **borderline resectable**. Corallo 2025's reproduction of "NCCN 2023" omits this clause. Check the NCCN v4.2026 PDF before printing.
8. **Taiwanese germline data differ from US data.** In a Taiwanese multicentre exome study (80 genes, n = 527), 19.7% carried a pathogenic or likely pathogenic (P/LP) germline variant; BRCA2 was the most common at 3.60%. The US Mayo series (6 genes) found 5.5%. The figures are not comparable, because the gene panels differ.
9. **CAPS 2020 did not reach consensus on universal germline testing** for all newly diagnosed PDAC, although NCCN recommends it. Do not write that "all guidelines agree".
10. **Smoking-cessation timing.** Iodice 2008: risk stays raised for **at least 10 years** after quitting. Bosetti 2012 (PanC4): risk reaches never-smoker level about **20 years** after quitting. Quote both correctly.
11. **New-onset diabetes ≥50 years.** About 1% (0.85%) are diagnosed with pancreatic cancer within 3 years (Chari 2005). CAPS 2020 cites 0.4% to 0.8%. "1 in 100" is the safe teaching figure.
12. **Not found or not openable (marked [UNVERIFIED] below):**
    - official HPA median age at diagnosis for pancreatic cancer in 2023 (www.hpa.gov.tw unreachable)
    - Taiwan 5-year survival for pancreatic cancer (the TCR survival report covers only the top 10 cancers)
    - PJS lifetime pancreatic-cancer cumulative risk percentage
    - percentage of patients with painless jaundice or a palpable gallbladder at presentation

## 3. High-risk surveillance: CAPS Consortium 2020 [VERIFIED; FT]

Source: Goggins et al., *Gut* 2020 (PMC7295005). Modified Delphi process; consensus required ≥75% agreement; 55 statements reached consensus.

### 3.1 Who qualifies (CAPS 2020, Table 1)

| Gene or family history | Family-history requirement for surveillance |
|---|---|
| **STK11/LKB1** (Peutz-Jeghers) | Regardless of family history |
| **CDKN2A** (p16, FAMMM) | Regardless of family history (77%); with ≥1 affected FDR (99%) |
| **BRCA2** | ≥1 affected FDR, **or** ≥2 affected relatives of any degree |
| **PALB2** | ≥1 affected FDR |
| **MLH1 / MSH2 / MSH6** (Lynch) | ≥1 affected FDR |
| **ATM** (newly added in 2020) | ≥1 affected FDR |
| **BRCA1** | ≥1 affected FDR. Agreement only 69.6%; another 20.3% "somewhat agreed". Surveillance itself reached consensus. |
| No known mutation (familial) | ≥3 affected relatives on the same side with ≥1 FDR of the person; **or** ≥2 affected relatives who are FDRs of each other with ≥1 FDR of the person; **or** ≥2 affected relatives on the same side with ≥1 FDR of the person |

"Relative" means blood relatives only.
- **Hereditary pancreatitis:** surveillance is recommended irrespective of gene status. Most experts favoured starting at age 40, or 20 years after the first pancreatitis attack.

### 3.2 When to start
- **Familial risk without a mutation:** no earlier than age 50, or 10 years younger than the youngest affected relative. Experts were split between age 50 and 55.
- **BRCA2, ATM and PALB2 carriers:** generally age 50; some groups start at 45.
- **CDKN2A carriers:** age 40.
- **Peutz-Jeghers syndrome:** age 30–40.
- No formal consensus was reached on starting ages for mutation carriers.
- **New-onset diabetes** in a high-risk individual should trigger surveillance or immediate investigation regardless of age.

### 3.3 How
- First-line tests are **EUS and MRI/MRCP**; no consensus was reached on how to alternate them.
- **CT is not first-line**: CAPS gives cumulative radiation as part of the reason, and reserves pancreatic-protocol CT for those who cannot have MRI or EUS. **Abdominal ultrasound is not recommended** as a first-line test.
- **Fasting glucose and/or HbA1c** should be checked routinely.
- **CA19-9 only when imaging shows worrisome features.** It is not a routine screening test.
- **Interval: every 12 months** if there are no concerning lesions. This also applies to small (<1 cm) non-functioning PanNETs and low-risk cysts.
- **CDKN2A carriers** with a concerning finding that does not lead to surgery should have repeat imaging in **3–6 months**.
- A solid lesion of uncertain significance that is not referred for surgery should be re-imaged in **3 months**.
- **Goal of surveillance:** detect **stage I (T1N0M0)** cancer and **high-grade dysplasia** (PanIN or IPMN).
- **Setting:** surveillance should be done in a research setting by expert multidisciplinary teams.
- **Smoking does not change the surveillance interval.**
- **Erratum:** *Gut* 2020;69(6):e3 (I did not check its content).

The NCCN Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Breast, Ovarian, Pancreatic, and Prostate guideline is at **Version 1.2027** on nccn.org (read 2026-10-29). It also has a pancreatic-screening section (see NCCN Insights v2.2026, Cheng et al. 2026 [A]). I did not read its detailed criteria: [PARTIAL].

## 4. Clinical presentation

| Feature | Evidence | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom frequencies at presentation | Prospective series, 185 Spanish patients: asthenia 86%, anorexia 85%, **weight loss 85%**, abdominal pain 79%, choluria (dark urine) 59%. Cholestatic symptoms were more common with head-only tumours. Body/tail tumours: none stage I, >80% stage IV. More cholestatic signs meant more localized tumours (Porta 2005 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| Symptoms vs controls | US population-based case-control study, ORs: appetite loss 41, pale stools 31, abdominal pain 30, **jaundice 20**, weight loss 12, itching 5.0 (Holly 2004 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| "Painless jaundice" | Head tumours present with cholestasis; body/tail tumours present late. A quotable percentage with *painless* jaundice was not found in an opened source. | [PARTIAL] |
| **Courvoisier's sign** (palpable, non-tender gallbladder plus jaundice) | Courvoisier described 187 cases of bile-duct obstruction in 1890. He observed that the gallbladder seldom dilates when a stone is the cause, and **never stated a "law"**. The modern explanation is chronic intraductal pressure in malignant obstruction, versus fibrosis or intermittent obstruction with stones (Fitzgerald 2009 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| | MRCP volumetry: gallbladder volume was larger in non-stone than in stone CBD obstruction (P = .03), supporting the sign (Murphy 2012 [A]). Sensitivity or frequency in PDAC: not found. | [VERIFIED / frequency UNVERIFIED] |
| **New-onset diabetes** | See §2: 47% of pancreatic cancer patients have DM, 74% of it new-onset; about 1% of people with NOD aged ≥50 develop cancer within 3 years. | [VERIFIED] |
| **Trousseau's syndrome** | Trousseau (1865) noted that unexpected or migratory thrombophlebitis can herald occult visceral cancer. The term is now used for anything from that classic picture to any cancer-associated coagulopathy (Varki 2007 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| VTE frequency in pancreatic cancer | Pooled VTE **5.9%** in chemotherapy RCTs (13 trials, 5,694 patients) vs **16.5%** in control arms of thromboprophylaxis trials. This shows under-reporting in chemotherapy trials (Chiasakul 2021 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| | Korean ambulatory chemotherapy cohort: pancreas 6-month cancer-associated thrombosis **4.16%** (20/481), lower than Western rates (Ha 2023 [FT]). | [VERIFIED] |
| Khorana score | Cancer site scores 2 points for a very-high-risk site; score ≥3 gave 6.7–7.1% VTE (Khorana 2008 [A]). Pancreas as a "very high-risk" site: the abstract does not list sites, and the full text was not opened. | [PARTIAL] |

Exam trap (editorial note, not sourced): **Trousseau's syndrome** (cancer-associated thrombosis) is different from **Trousseau's sign** (carpopedal spasm with a BP cuff in hypocalcaemia).

| | Chinese PDAC cohort (FUT3 genotyping, n = 682): **6.9%** Lewis-negative. In these patients, CEA (63.8%) and CA125 (51.1%) were more sensitive than CA19-9 (19.1%) (Luo 2017 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| | Japanese resected PDAC (n = 2,418): **7.7%** were CA19-9 non-expressors (≤2 U/mL); DUPAN-2 was a usable surrogate (Omiya 2026 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| **False positives in cholestasis** | Obstructive jaundice raises false positivity to **10–60%** (Ballehaninna 2012 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| | Elevated values should be **repeated after relief of jaundice** (Goonetilleke 2007 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |
| | NCCN Patients 2026: high CA19-9 can come from a blocked bile duct and certain medicines; some patients "don't make CA 19-9"; blood tests should be taken when the bile ducts are clear. If CA19-9 is normal, CEA and CA-125 may be checked [NCCN patient guideline]. | [VERIFIED] |
| Prognosis | Pre-op CA19-9 <37 U/mL: median survival 32–36 months, vs 12–15 months if >37. A level **>100 U/mL suggests unresectable or metastatic disease**. A post-treatment fall of ≥20–50% predicts longer survival (Ballehaninna 2012 [A]). | [VERIFIED] |

Ward tip from the evidence above: draw baseline CA19-9 **after biliary drainage, once bilirubin is falling**. A "normal" CA19-9 does not exclude PDAC in a Lewis-negative patient.

## 6. Imaging and tissue diagnosis

### 6.1 Pancreas-protocol CT/MRI

- **NCCN** recommends a **pancreatic-protocol CT or MRI** of the abdomen [NCCN Patients 2026]. **[VERIFIED]**
  - Everyone also needs **contrast CT of the chest and pelvis**.
  - Optional: MRI of the liver if the CT is unclear; PET/CT or PET/MRI if distant spread is likely; EUS, ERCP or laparoscopy; liver function tests and CA19-9.
- **SAR/APA protocol** (dual-phase CT; Al-Hawary 2014 consensus, as summarized by Chu & Fishman 2024 [FT]) **[PARTIAL]**, secondary summary of the SAR/APA consensus; the consensus full text was not opened:
  - IV contrast >300 mg I/mL at 3–5 mL/s
  - **pancreatic parenchymal phase 40–50 s** and **portal venous phase 65–70 s**
  - neutral or low-HU oral contrast
  - **submillimetre acquisition**, reconstructed at 0.75–3 mm
  - multiplanar and 3D reconstruction to assess the vessels
- **Image before stenting.** CT should precede biliary stent placement, because stent artefact or post-ERCP pancreatitis can obscure staging (Corallo 2025 [FT]). **[VERIFIED]** (review statement)
- Al-Hawary 2014 standardized reporting template (SAR/APA): abstract read. **[VERIFIED citation]**

### 6.2 EUS-guided biopsy (EUS-FNB vs FNA)

- **NCCN Patients 2026:**
  - The **preferred method of needle biopsy is EUS-guided**, because it gets better samples with less risk. If an ERCP for a stent is planned, biopsy can be done first.
  - A biopsy is required before chemotherapy if surgery is not first.
  - Several samples should be taken, enough for both diagnosis and biomarker testing.
  - Percutaneous CT- or US-guided biopsy is used when metastases are suspected.
  - NCCN does not recommend ctDNA testing for diagnosis.
  - **[VERIFIED]**
- **FNB vs FNA**, meta-analysis of 18 RCTs: FNB had higher diagnostic accuracy (**87% vs 80%**, P = 0.02), a higher tissue-core rate (80% vs 62%) and needed fewer passes (van Riet 2021 [A]). **[VERIFIED]**
- Meta-analysis of 12 prospective studies in solid pancreatic masses: FNA had lower accuracy (risk difference −0.08) and lower specimen adequacy than FNB, with similar complication rates (Yao 2024 [A]). **[VERIFIED]**
- EUS biopsy sensitivity 90.8%, specificity 96.5%, complications 0.03–4%. Less peritoneal seeding than the percutaneous route (Corallo 2025 [FT], citing primary studies). **[PARTIAL]**

## 7. NCCN germline and somatic (tumour) testing

### 7.1 Germline testing for ALL PDAC [VERIFIED via NCCN Patients 2026; professional wording PARTIAL]

- NCCN Patients 2026 (based on professional v2.2026): "**All people with pancreatic cancer should get genetic testing**", using blood or saliva.
- Germline genes listed by NCCN: **ATM, BRCA1, BRCA2, CDKN2A, PALB2, MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, STK11, TP53**.
- Genetic counselling is recommended for carriers or for a strong family history.
- CAPS 2020 [FT] confirms that "recent NCCN guidelines recommend offering gene testing for patients with newly diagnosed PDAC". **This recommendation did not reach consensus among CAPS experts.**
- **ASCO 2019 provisional clinical opinion** [A]: all patients with pancreatic adenocarcinoma should have a hereditary-risk assessment including family history. Germline testing "may be discussed" even if the family history is unremarkable. **[VERIFIED]**
- Why it matters: the germline result drives treatment. It identifies candidates for platinum (Cheng 2024) and for olaparib maintenance in gBRCA (POLO; Golan 2019). It also triggers cascade testing and surveillance of relatives.

### 7.2 Somatic (tumour) profiling [VERIFIED via NCCN Patients 2026]

- NCCN Patients 2026: "Pancreatic cancer that **will be treated with prescription drugs** should be tested for biomarkers that affect treatment decisions."
  - Test many markers at once.
  - Tumour tissue is preferred; blood (ctDNA) is used if tissue testing is not possible.
- Biomarkers listed by NCCN (Guide 3):

| Alteration type | Targets with a treatment option |
|---|---|
| **Fusions** | ALK, NRG1, NTRK, ROS1, FGFR2, RET |
| **Mutations** | BRAF, BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, **KRAS** (G12C: adagrasib, sotorasib) |
| **Amplification / overexpression** | HER2 |
| **Genome-wide** | MSI-H, dMMR, TMB-H |

- Kinase inhibitors named in the patient guide:
  - erlotinib (EGFR)
  - entrectinib, larotrectinib, repotrectinib (TRK)
  - dabrafenib + trametinib (BRAF V600E)
  - selpercatinib (RET)
  - adagrasib, sotorasib (KRAS G12C)
  - erdafitinib (FGFR)
- Other options named in the patient guide:
  - zenocutuzumab-zbco, an antibody against HER2/HER3 (used for NRG1 fusions)
  - fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan (HER2)
  - PARP inhibitors olaparib and rucaparib
  - checkpoint inhibitors pembrolizumab, nivolumab, dostarlimab and ipilimumab (for MSI-H/dMMR/TMB-H)
  - The NRG1 link for zenocutuzumab is inferred from Guide 3; the patient guide does not pair them explicitly.
- Drug-level details belong in the treatment notes and should be checked against TFDA approvals.
- Professional-guideline wording (for example "tumor/somatic gene profiling is recommended for patients with locally advanced/metastatic disease who are candidates for anticancer therapy"): **[PARTIAL]**. It is consistent with the patient guide, but the PDF was not opened.

## 8. AJCC 8th edition TNM: exocrine pancreas [PARTIAL]

Status:
- The AJCC "Current Staging System 2026" chart lists Chapter 28 **Exocrine Pancreas as 8th edition** (no Version 9 yet), and Chapter 34 **NET Pancreas as Version 9 (2024)**. **[VERIFIED]** https://www.facs.org/media/c5ik5tkr/ajcc-current-staging-system-2026.pdf
- AJCC 8th took effect **2018-01-01** (Shah 2020 [FT]). **[VERIFIED]** (secondary)
- The AJCC manual itself was not opened. The TNM text below is from Allen 2017 [FT; proposed 8th edition], Rhee & Park 2021 [FT] and Corallo 2025 [FT]. All three agree, so the tag is **[PARTIAL; three-source agreement]**.

**T (based on size; extrapancreatic extension was removed in the 8th edition)**

| T | Definition |
|---|---|
| Tis | Carcinoma in situ, including high-grade PanIN, and IPMN, ITPN or MCN with high-grade dysplasia |
| T1 | **≤2 cm**. T1a ≤0.5 cm; T1b >0.5 and <1 cm; T1c 1–2 cm |
| T2 | **>2 cm and ≤4 cm** |
| T3 | **>4 cm** |
| T4 | Involves the **celiac axis, SMA and/or common hepatic artery**, regardless of size |

**N (based on number of positive nodes)**

| N | Definition |
|---|---|
| N0 | No regional node metastasis |
| N1 | **1–3** positive regional nodes |
| N2 | **≥4** positive regional nodes |

**M:** M0 = no distant metastasis; M1 = distant metastasis.

**Stage groups**

| Stage | TNM |
|---|---|
| 0 | Tis N0 M0 |
| IA | T1 N0 M0 |
| IB | T2 N0 M0 |
| IIA | T3 N0 M0 |
| IIB | T1–3 **N1** M0 |
| III | **Any T N2** M0, **or T4 any N** M0 |
| IV | Any T, any N, **M1** |

Changes from the 7th edition (Rhee & Park 2021 [FT]):
- T is now size-based.
- T4 no longer says "unresectable" and now includes the CHA.
- N1 was split into N1 and N2.
- T1–3 N2 moved to stage III.
- IPMN, ITPN and MCN with high-grade dysplasia were added to Tis.

Validation:
- Allen 2017 [A], 2,318 R0 resections: recursive partitioning found size cut-offs of 2.2 and 4.8 cm, and node cut-offs of 0, 1–3 and ≥4, supporting the 8th edition. Concordance 0.58 vs 0.57 for the 7th edition.
- van Roessel 2018 [A], n = 1,525: 5-year OS was 35.6% for N0, **20.8% for N1** and **10.9% for N2**. N stage was strongly prognostic, but T stage was not prognostic in node-negative patients. C-statistic 0.57 vs 0.55.
- **[VERIFIED]**

Teaching point: AJCC stage is pathological and prognostic. **Treatment is decided by NCCN resectability (§9)**, not by AJCC stage.

## 9. NCCN resectability definitions [PARTIAL]

Sources:
- Corallo 2025 Table 2 [FT], which reproduces "NCCN 2023" criteria nearly verbatim
- Chu & Fishman 2024 Table 1 [FT]
- Søreide & Jamieson 2026 [FT], for the Appleby footnote

The NCCN PDF itself (v4.2026) was not opened. NCCN Patients 2026 confirms the three categories (resectable, borderline resectable, locally advanced) and that the decision is made by a multidisciplinary team, but gives no degree thresholds.

| Status | Arterial (CA, SMA, CHA) | Venous (SMV, PV) |
|---|---|---|
| **Resectable** | **No arterial tumour contact** (CA, SMA or CHA) | No contact with SMV/PV, **or ≤180° contact without vein contour irregularity** |
| **Borderline resectable**, head/uncinate | Solid tumour contact with the **CHA** without extension to the CA or hepatic artery bifurcation, allowing safe complete resection and reconstruction. Solid tumour contact with the **SMA ≤180°**. Contact with variant arterial anatomy (accessory or replaced right hepatic artery, replaced CHA, origin of a replaced or accessory artery): note its presence and degree. | Contact with SMV/PV **>180°**, **or ≤180° with contour irregularity or thrombosis**, with suitable vessel above and below to allow safe resection and reconstruction. Solid tumour contact with the **IVC**. |
| **Borderline resectable**, body/tail | Solid tumour contact with the **CA ≤180°**. Also, per Søreide 2026: CA **>180°** *without aortic involvement* and with an intact, uninvolved GDA, permitting a **modified Appleby procedure**. This clause is absent from the Corallo 2025 reproduction; verify against v4.2026. | (as above) |
| **Locally advanced**, head/uncinate | Solid tumour contact **>180° with the SMA or CA** | **Unreconstructible SMV/PV** due to tumour involvement or occlusion (by tumour or bland thrombus) |
| **Locally advanced**, body/tail | Contact **>180° with the SMA or CA**. Contact with the **CA and aortic involvement**. | (as above) |

NCCN footnotes, as reproduced by Corallo 2025:
- Solid tumour contact may be replaced by **hazy increased density or stranding** of the perivascular fat after neoadjuvant therapy; report it.
- **Distant metastasis, including non-regional nodes, means metastatic disease** regardless of anatomic resectability, and upfront resection should not be done.

Contrast with the Dutch Pancreatic Cancer Group criteria (Chu 2024; Søreide 2026):
- Resectable: venous contact ≤**90°**.
- Borderline: arterial ≤90°, or venous >90–270° without occlusion.
- Locally advanced: arterial **>90°**, or venous **>270°** or occlusion.

Do not mix the NCCN and Dutch systems in one table.

Memory aid (editorial): **artery ≤180° = borderline; artery >180° = locally advanced; any reconstructable vein = borderline; unreconstructable vein = locally advanced.**

### CA-2. Taiwan screening policy (§1 "screening")

- Taiwan's publicly funded programme covers **five cancers only** [VERIFIED; MOHW press release 2025-12-30]:
  - oral: mucosal exam every 2 years
  - colorectal: FIT every 2 years, age 45–74, or 40–44 with a family history
  - cervical
  - breast: mammography every 2 years, age 40–74
  - lung: LDCT every 2 years, for men 45–74 and women 40–74 with a family history, and heavy smokers aged 50–74 with ≥20 pack-years
- **There is no population screening for pancreatic cancer.** High-risk surveillance follows CAPS 2020 (§3) and the Pan-Asian guideline below.
- **Pan-Asian ESMO 2025** (Taiwan Oncology Society voted) [VERIFIED; FT]:
  - Recommendation 1c: surveillance of high-risk individuals in **expert centres**, usually starting at **age 50, or 10 years younger than the youngest affected relative** [III, A].
  - Recommendation 1d: **annual EUS and/or pancreatic MRI** preferred [IV, B].
  - Recommendation 2a: multiphasic contrast CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis, including late arterial and portal venous phases, as first-line imaging [III, A].
  - Recommendation 2b: **imaging before biliary drainage or stenting** in jaundiced head cancers [IV, A]. This upgrades §6.1 "image before stenting" from a review statement to a guideline recommendation.
  - Recommendation 2c: imaging within **4 weeks** before starting treatment [III, A].

### CA-3. Precursor lesions and pancreatic cysts (pathology section; previously absent)

**WHO 2019 (5th edition)** (Nagtegaal 2020) [VERIFIED; FT]
- PanIN, IPMN and MCN now use a **two-tier grade (low-grade vs high-grade dysplasia)**, based on the highest grade present. This replaces the old three-tier PanIN-1/2/3 system.
- "Carcinoma in situ" is discouraged; it falls under high-grade dysplasia.
- Intraductal oncocytic papillary neoplasm and ITPN are now separate from IPMN.
- Exam trap: "PanIN-3" is the outdated term.

**Kyoto 2024 international IPMN guideline** (Ohtsuka 2024): abstract [VERIFIED]; criteria lists [PARTIAL], taken from Vara-Luiz 2025, which tabulates Kyoto 2024 [FT]
- What the abstract confirms:
  - It revises the 2017 Fukuoka guideline.
  - High-risk stigmata (HRS) and worrisome features (WF) now incorporate **EUS findings and EUS-FNA cytology**.
  - For **small BD-IPMN unchanged after 5 years** of surveillance there are two options: stop surveillance, or continue for possible concomitant PDAC.
- **High-risk stigmata (HRS):**
  - obstructive jaundice in a patient with a cystic lesion of the pancreatic head
  - enhancing mural nodule ≥5 mm, or a solid component
  - **main pancreatic duct ≥10 mm**
  - suspicious or positive cytology (high-grade dysplasia or adenocarcinoma), newly added since Fukuoka
- **Worrisome features (WF):**
  - acute pancreatitis
  - raised serum CA19-9
  - **new-onset or acute exacerbation of diabetes within the past year** (new)
  - cyst ≥30 mm
  - enhancing mural nodule <5 mm
  - thickened or enhancing cyst wall
  - MPD 5–9.9 mm
  - abrupt change in MPD calibre with distal atrophy
  - lymphadenopathy
  - **cyst growth ≥2.5 mm/year** (Fukuoka 2017 used ≥5 mm/2 years)
- Risk of high-grade dysplasia or invasive cancer rises with the number of WF (as cited by Vara-Luiz): **22%, 34% and 59%** with 1, 2 and 3 WF, and **100% with ≥4**.
- **Surveillance of non-resected IPMN** (Kyoto, as tabulated):

| Cyst size | Interval |
|---|---|
| <20 mm | at 6 months once, then **every 18 months** if stable |
| 20–<30 mm | at 6 months twice, then **every 12 months** |
| ≥30 mm without another WF | **every 6 months** |

  - Tests: clinical review, **CA19-9, HbA1c**, MRI. Add CT or EUS if the MRI changes.
- **Natural history:**
  - Indolent BD-IPMN progresses to high-grade dysplasia or invasive cancer in **0.94–3.3% by 5 years**, 2.3–6.6% by 10 years and 7.6–15% by 15 years.
  - The risk of concomitant PDAC elsewhere in the gland is about **3–5× the age-matched population** and persists after 5 years ("dual carcinogenesis").
  - Multifocal BD-IPMN (20–40%) does not by itself raise risk.
- NP relevance: new-onset diabetes is now a WF in IPMN surveillance. This links to §2 (new-onset diabetes as an early sign of PDAC). An HbA1c check belongs in the cyst-surveillance order set.
