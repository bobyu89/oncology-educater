# B2 — Lung cancer chapter: verification of NSCLC early-stage, NSCLC immunotherapy/chemotherapy and SCLC trials

**Verification date:** 2026-10-29
**Purpose:** Citation and data check for the lung cancer chapter of the Taiwanese oncology nurse practitioner handbook.
**Sources searched:** PubMed (MCP PubMed server plus NCBI E-utilities esearch/efetch, metadata pulled straight from the PubMed XML), PubMed Central full text (where a PMCID exists), Crossref (one title check) and FDA prescribing information from openFDA (label text, used only where a published abstract left out a dose or an HR; every such use is marked **[FDA label]**).

## How to read this file

- **Section tags**
  - **[VERIFIED]**: the citation (authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages and DOI) matches PubMed, and the primary-endpoint effect estimate was read from the primary source, or from the FDA label where that is stated.
  - **[PARTIAL]**: the citation is confirmed, but the primary effect estimate or another core item is missing from every source that could be reached.
  - **[UNVERIFIED]**: the citation itself could not be confirmed. No section carries this tag.
- **Inline tag [UNVERIFIED]:** that number or detail does not appear in the abstract, PMC full text or FDA label I checked. It must not be filled in from memory; check the full paper first.
- **Wording and source of numbers:** numbers are quoted as the papers report them. Unless another source is named (PMC full text, or FDA label), every number comes from the PubMed abstract.
- **Non-95% confidence intervals:** where a paper reports one (for example 97.38% or 98.321%), the level is kept as reported.
- **APA 7 author lists:** 1–20 authors are all listed. With 21 or more, the first 19 are listed, then ". . .", then the last *individual* author. Collective names such as "for the X Investigators" are not counted as authors, except for NLST, where PubMed lists the Research Team first.
- **APA 7 titles and journals:** article titles are converted to sentence case, and journal names are written out in full.

# Part A. Screening and early-stage NSCLC

## A1. NLST: low-dose CT screening [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** National Lung Screening Trial Research Team, Aberle, D. R., Adams, A. M., Berg, C. D., Black, W. C., Clapp, J. D., Fagerstrom, R. M., Gareen, I. F., Gatsonis, C., Marcus, P. M., & Sicks, J. D. (2011). Reduced lung-cancer mortality with low-dose computed tomographic screening. *New England Journal of Medicine, 365*(5), 395–409. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1102873
PMID 21714641 · PMCID PMC4356534

- **Design:** randomized screening trial at 33 U.S. centres. Enrolment ran August 2002 to April 2004, with follow-up to Dec 31, 2009. The PubMed record gives no trial phase; phase is not applicable to a screening RCT.
- **Population:** 53,454 people at high risk.
  - Eligibility (PMC full text): age 55–74, at least 30 pack-years, and former smokers had to have quit within the past 15 years.
- **Arms:** three annual screens.
  - Low-dose CT: n = 26,722.
  - Single-view PA chest radiography: n = 26,732.
- **Primary endpoint, lung cancer mortality:**
  - 247 deaths per 100,000 person-years with LDCT vs 309 with CXR.
  - Relative reduction **20.0% (95% CI 6.8–26.7; P = 0.004)**.
- **Secondary results:**
  - All-cause mortality fell by 6.7% (95% CI 1.2–13.6; P = 0.02).
  - Adherence was above 90%.
  - Positive-screen rate over three rounds: 24.2% (LDCT) vs 6.9% (CXR).
  - **False-positive share of positive screens: 96.4% vs 94.5%.**
  - Incidence: 645 vs 572 per 100,000 person-years (rate ratio 1.13; 95% CI 1.03–1.23).
- **Harms signal:** the main harm is the very high false-positive rate. Overdiagnosis and complications of work-up are not quantified in the abstract **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## A2. NELSON: volume CT screening [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** de Koning, H. J., van der Aalst, C. M., de Jong, P. A., Scholten, E. T., Nackaerts, K., Heuvelmans, M. A., Lammers, J. J., Weenink, C., Yousaf-Khan, U., Horeweg, N., van 't Westeinde, S., Prokop, M., Mali, W. P., Mohamed Hoesein, F. A. A., van Ooijen, P. M. A., Aerts, J. G. J. V., den Bakker, M. A., Thunnissen, E., Verschakelen, J., . . . Oudkerk, M. (2020). Reduced lung-cancer mortality with volume CT screening in a randomized trial. *New England Journal of Medicine, 382*(6), 503–513. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1911793
PMID 31995683

- **Design:** randomized screening trial; outcomes came from national registry linkage in the Netherlands and Belgium. The PubMed record gives no phase.
- **Population:**
  - 13,195 men (primary analysis) and 2,594 women (subgroup), aged 50–74.
  - The smoking-history eligibility criteria are not in the abstract **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Arms:** volume CT at baseline, year 1, year 3 and year 5.5, vs no screening. Minimum follow-up was 10 years.
- **Primary endpoint (men, 10-year lung cancer mortality):**
  - Cumulative rate ratio **0.76 (95% CI 0.61–0.94; P = 0.01)**.
  - 2.50 vs 3.30 deaths per 1,000 person-years.
- **Secondary results:**
  - Women: rate ratio 0.67 (95% CI 0.38–1.14) at 10 years, which is **not statistically significant**; years 7–9 ranged 0.41–0.52.
  - Incidence: 5.58 vs 4.91 per 1,000 person-years.
- **Harms and work-up burden:**
  - Adherence 90.0%.
  - 9.2% of screened participants needed at least one extra CT for an indeterminate result.
  - **Referral rate for suspicious nodules: 2.1%**, much lower than NLST's positive-screen rate.

## A3. JCOG0802/WJOG4607L: segmentectomy vs lobectomy [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** Saji, H., Okada, M., Tsuboi, M., Nakajima, R., Suzuki, K., Aokage, K., Aoki, T., Okami, J., Yoshino, I., Ito, H., Okumura, N., Yamaguchi, M., Ikeda, N., Wakabayashi, M., Nakamura, K., Fukuda, H., Nakamura, S., Mitsudomi, T., Watanabe, S. I., & Asamura, H. (2022). Segmentectomy versus lobectomy in small-sized peripheral non-small-cell lung cancer (JCOG0802/WJOG4607L): A multicentre, open-label, phase 3, randomised, controlled, non-inferiority trial. *The Lancet, 399*(10335), 1607–1617. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)02333-3
PMID 35461558

- **Design:** phase 3, randomized, open-label, non-inferiority trial at 70 Japanese institutions.
  - Non-inferiority margin: upper 95% CI bound of the HR at 1.54.
- **Population:** 1,106 patients (ITT) with clinical stage IA peripheral NSCLC, tumour ≤ 2 cm and consolidation-to-tumour ratio > 0.5.
- **Arms:** lobectomy (n = 554) vs segmentectomy (n = 552). In the segmentectomy arm, 22 patients were converted to lobectomy.
- **Primary endpoint, OS (median follow-up 7.3 years):**
  - 5-year OS **94.3% (segmentectomy) vs 91.1% (lobectomy)**.
  - **HR 0.663 (95% CI 0.474–0.927)**.
  - Non-inferiority: one-sided p < 0.0001. Superiority: **p = 0.0082**, so segmentectomy was *superior*.
- **Secondary results:**
  - 5-year RFS 88.0% vs 87.9% (HR 0.998; 95% CI 0.753–1.323).
  - **Local relapse 10.5% (segmentectomy) vs 5.4% (lobectomy), p = 0.0018.**
  - FEV1 reduction at 1 year differed by 3.5%. This is statistically significant (p < 0.0001) but below the 10% threshold set for clinical relevance.
  - Deaths from other diseases: 63% after lobectomy vs 47% after segmentectomy.
- **Toxicity:**
  - No 30-day or 90-day mortality.
  - Grade ≥ 2 postoperative complications: 26% (lobectomy) vs 27% (segmentectomy).

## A4. CALGB 140503 (Alliance): sublobar vs lobar resection [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** Altorki, N., Wang, X., Kozono, D., Watt, C., Landrenau, R., Wigle, D., Port, J., Jones, D. R., Conti, M., Ashrafi, A. S., Liberman, M., Yasufuku, K., Yang, S., Mitchell, J. D., Pass, H., Keenan, R., Bauer, T., Miller, D., Kohman, L. J., . . . Vokes, E. (2023). Lobar or sublobar resection for peripheral stage IA non-small-cell lung cancer. *New England Journal of Medicine, 388*(6), 489–498. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2212083
PMID 36780674 · PMCID PMC10036605

- **Design:** phase 3, multicentre, non-inferiority trial.
- **Population:** 697 patients with peripheral NSCLC, clinical T1aN0, tumour ≤ 2 cm.
  - Randomization took place **during surgery, after node-negative status was confirmed** in hilar and mediastinal nodes.
- **Arms:** sublobar resection (n = 340) vs lobectomy (n = 357). Enrolment ran June 2007 to March 2017.
- **Primary endpoint, DFS (median follow-up 7 years):**
  - **HR 1.01, reported with a 90% CI (0.83–1.24)**. The interval is 90%, not 95%.
  - 5-year DFS 63.6% vs 64.1%.
- **Secondary results:**
  - OS HR 0.95 (95% CI 0.72–1.26).
  - 5-year OS 80.3% vs 78.9%.
  - Locoregional and distant recurrence did not differ substantially.
  - At 6 months, median % predicted FEV1 favoured sublobar resection by 2 percentage points.
- **Toxicity:** perioperative complication rates are not in the abstract **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Note:** the sublobar arm included wedge resection or segmentectomy. The abstract does not give the split **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## A5. LACE: adjuvant cisplatin individual-patient-data pooled analysis [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** Pignon, J. P., Tribodet, H., Scagliotti, G. V., Douillard, J. Y., Shepherd, F. A., Stephens, R. J., Dunant, A., Torri, V., Rosell, R., Seymour, L., Spiro, S. Lung adjuvant cisplatin evaluation: A pooled analysis by the LACE Collaborative Group. *Journal of Clinical Oncology, 26*(21), 3552–3559. https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2007.13.9030
PMID 18506026

- **Design:** individual-patient-data pooled analysis of the 5 largest cisplatin-based adjuvant trials run after 1995. The title calls it a "pooled analysis", not a meta-analysis.
- **Population:** 4,584 patients with completely resected NSCLC. Median follow-up was 5.2 years.
- **Primary result, OS:**
  - **HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.82–0.96; P = .005)**.
  - **Absolute 5-year benefit 5.4%**.
  - No heterogeneity between trials.
- **By stage (test for trend P = .04):**

  | Stage | HR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|
  | IA | **1.40 (0.95–2.06)**: possible harm |
  | IB | 0.93 (0.78–1.10) |
  | II | 0.83 (0.73–0.95) |
  | III | 0.83 (0.72–0.94) |

- **By partner drug (interaction P = .11):**
  - Vinorelbine: HR 0.80 (0.70–0.91).
  - Etoposide or vinca alkaloid: 0.92 (0.80–1.07).
  - Other: 0.97 (0.84–1.13).
- **Other subgroups:**
  - The benefit was larger with better performance status.
  - There was no interaction with sex, age, histology, type of surgery, planned RT or planned cisplatin dose.
- **Toxicity:** chemotherapy toxicity and toxic death rates are not in the abstract **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## A7. PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091: adjuvant pembrolizumab [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** O'Brien, M., Paz-Ares, L., Marreaud, S., Dafni, U., Oselin, K., Havel, L., Esteban, E., Isla, D., Martinez-Marti, A., Faehling, M., Tsuboi, M., Lee, J. S., Nakagawa, K., Yang, J., Samkari, A., Keller, S. M., Mauer, M., Jha, N., Stahel, R., . . . Peters, S. (2022). Pembrolizumab versus placebo as adjuvant therapy for completely resected stage IB-IIIA non-small-cell lung cancer (PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091): An interim analysis of a randomised, triple-blind, phase 3 trial. *The Lancet Oncology, 23*(10), 1274–1286. https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(22)00518-6
PMID 36108662

- **Design:** phase 3, randomized, **triple-blind**, placebo-controlled trial at 196 centres in 29 countries. This is the second prespecified interim analysis.
- **Population:**
  - Completely resected stage IB (≥ 4 cm), II or IIIA NSCLC (AJCC 7th edition), any PD-L1 level, ECOG 0–1.
  - Adjuvant chemotherapy was to be considered for stage IB and strongly recommended for stage II–IIIA.
- **Arms:**
  - Pembrolizumab **200 mg IV every 3 weeks for up to 18 cycles** (n = 590).
  - Placebo (n = 587).
- **Dual primary endpoint, DFS (median follow-up 35.6 months):**
  - **Overall population:** median **53.6 months (95% CI 39.2–NR) vs 42.0 months (31.3–NR)**; **HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.63–0.91), p = 0.0014**.
  - **PD-L1 TPS ≥ 50%:** median not reached in either arm; **HR 0.82 (0.57–1.18), p = 0.14. Not significant at this interim analysis.**
- **Secondary results:** OS is not reported in the abstract **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Toxicity:**
  - Grade ≥ 3 AEs 34% vs 26%.
  - Serious AEs 24% vs 15%: pneumonia 2%, pneumonitis 2%, diarrhoea 1%.
  - **Four treatment-related deaths with pembrolizumab:** two involved myocarditis, plus one pneumonia and one sudden death.

## A8. CheckMate 816: neoadjuvant nivolumab plus chemotherapy [VERIFIED]

**Primary reference (EFS/pCR):** Forde, P. M., Spicer, J., Lu, S., Provencio, M., Mitsudomi, T., Awad, M. M., Felip, E., Broderick, S. R., Brahmer, J. R., Swanson, S. **Overall survival reference (published 2025):** Forde, P. M., Spicer, J. D., Provencio, M., Mitsudomi, T., Awad, M. M., Wang, C., Lu, S., Felip, E., Swanson, S. J., Brahmer, J. R., Kerr, K., Taube, J. M., Ciuleanu, T. E., Tanaka, F., Saylors, G. B., Chen, K. N., Ito, H., Liberman, M., Martin, C., . . . Girard, N. (2025). PMID 40454642

- **Design:** phase 3, open-label.
- **Population:** resectable stage IB (≥ 4 cm) to IIIA NSCLC, **AJCC 7th edition**, ECOG 0–1; concurrently randomized n = 358 (179 per arm; figures from the 2025 abstract).
  - PMC full text: patients with known EGFR mutations or ALK translocations were excluded.
- **Arms** (PMC full text):
  - **Nivolumab 360 mg + platinum doublet every 3 weeks for 3 cycles** vs platinum doublet alone for 3 cycles, both followed by surgery.
  - A third arm, nivolumab + ipilimumab, closed early.
- **Primary endpoints (blinded independent review):**
  - **EFS:** median **31.6 months (95% CI 30.2–NR) vs 20.8 months (14.0–26.7)**; **HR 0.63 (97.38% CI 0.43–0.91), P = 0.005**.
  - **pCR:** **24.0% vs 2.2%** (OR 13.94; 99% CI 3.49–55.75; P < 0.001).

  - Definitive surgery: 83.2% vs 75.4%.

  - **HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.523–0.998; P = 0.048)**. The upper CI bound is only just below 1.
  - Median follow-up 68.4 months.
  - **5-year OS 65.4% vs 55.0%.**
  - Exploratory 5-year OS in the nivolumab arm:
    - With pCR: 95.3% (95% CI 82.7–98.8).
    - Without pCR: 55.7% (46.9–63.7).
    - With presurgery ctDNA clearance: 75.0%.
    - Without ctDNA clearance: 52.6%.
  - No new safety signals.

- **Three-year update (Paz-Ares 2022 ESMO Open; median follow-up 39.4 months):**

## C7. Lurbinectedin, second line: Trigo 2020 basket trial [VERIFIED]

## C8. DeLLphi-301: tarlatamab, previously treated SCLC [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** Ahn, M. J., Cho, B. C., Felip, E., Korantzis, I., Ohashi, K., Majem, M., Juan-Vidal, O., Handzhiev, S., Izumi, H., Lee, J. PMID 37861218

- **Design:** phase 2 trial comparing two doses.
- **Population:** 220 patients with previously treated SCLC; median 2 prior lines.
- **Arms:**
  - **Tarlatamab 10 mg or 100 mg IV every 2 weeks.**
  - The step-up dose is not in the abstract. The FDA label regimen is 1 mg on cycle 1 day 1, then 10 mg on days 8 and 15 and every 2 weeks after **[FDA label]**.
- **Primary endpoint, ORR by blinded independent central review:**
  - **10 mg: 40% (97.5% CI 29–52).**
  - 100 mg: 32% (97.5% CI 21–44).
  - Note the 97.5% CIs.
- **Secondary results (median follow-up 10.6 months at 10 mg, 10.3 months at 100 mg):**
  - Response lasted ≥ 6 months in 59% (40/68) of responders.
  - Median PFS 4.9 months (10 mg) vs 3.9 months (100 mg).
  - 9-month OS 68% vs 66%.
- **Toxicity:**
  - **CRS 51% (10 mg) vs 61% (100 mg)**, mostly in cycle 1 and mostly grade 1–2. **Grade 3 CRS 1% vs 6%.**
  - Decreased appetite 29% vs 44%.
  - Pyrexia 35% vs 33%.
  - Discontinuation for treatment-related AEs: 3%.
  - ICANS rates are not in the abstract. The FDA label (pooled tarlatamab safety population) reports:
    - CRS 57% (268/473): grade 1 39%, grade 2 15%, grade 3 1.7%, grade 4 0.2%.
    - ICANS-consistent events 10%, including one fatal case.
    - The label carries a **boxed warning for CRS and neurologic toxicity, including ICANS**.

## C10. IMforte: lurbinectedin plus atezolizumab maintenance, ES-SCLC [VERIFIED]

**Reference:** Paz-Ares, L., Borghaei, H., Liu, S. V., Peters, S., Herbst, R. S., Stencel, K., Majem, M., Şendur, M. A. N., Czyżewicz, G., Caro, R. B., Lee, K. H., Johnson, M. PMID 40473449

- **Design:** phase 3, randomized, open-label, at 96 hospitals in 13 countries. **Taiwan was one of the countries**, and Taichung Veterans General Hospital is a co-author site.
- **Population:**
  - 660 patients with treatment-naive ES-SCLC entered induction: 4 × 21-day cycles of atezolizumab + carboplatin + etoposide.
  - **483 without progression were randomized** to maintenance (242 vs 241).
- **Arms, IV every 3 weeks:**
  - **Lurbinectedin 3.2 mg/m² with G-CSF prophylaxis + atezolizumab 1,200 mg.**
  - **Atezolizumab 1,200 mg** alone.
- **Primary endpoints, measured from randomization into maintenance (cutoff Jul 29, 2024):**
  - **PFS by independent review: HR 0.54 (95% CI 0.43–0.67); p < 0.0001.**

- **Toxicity:**
  - **Grade 3–4 AEs 38% vs 22%.**
  - Anaemia 8%, decreased neutrophils 7%, decreased platelets 7%.
  - Grade 5 AEs 5% vs 3%.
  - **Myelosuppression was more frequent with lurbinectedin.**
