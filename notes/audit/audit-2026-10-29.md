# 肺癌與共用章節查核紀錄（2026-10-29）

流程：17 個分段查核＋1 個跨章一致性檢查 → 每個疑點由「原始文獻」與「懷疑論臨床」兩位驗證者獨立判斷 → 兩者都認定是錯誤才修改。

結果：確認並已修正 65 項；意見分歧未修改 8 項；判定無誤 1 項。

## 已修正（依嚴重度）

- **[critical]** `handbook/00-shared/03-supportive-care.md`：This understates the contraindication. The label limits transdermal fentanyl to opioid-tolerant patients and contraindicates it in every patient who is not opioid-tolerant, not only opioid-naive ones. Patients on low doses (for example morphine 20 mg/day, or tramadol) are not opioid-naive, yet the patch is still contraindicated for them. A learner who reads the current line could start a 12–25 µg/h patch in a patient who is not tolerant, with a risk of fatal respiratory depression (boxed warning).
- **[critical]** `handbook/01-lung-cancer.md`：The SIADH row lists seizures and confusion under 辨識, but the 立即處置 column gives only fluid restriction, salt tablets and tolvaptan. It leaves out the emergency step for severe symptomatic hyponatremia: an immediate 3% hypertonic saline bolus. Fluid restriction and salt tablets act over days, so a seizing patient managed from this row would get no acute treatment. The shared emergencies chapter (00-shared/02-oncologic-emergencies.md, lines 32 and 312) does say '有嚴重症狀 → 3% NaCl', so the lung table also contradicts the handbook's own shared chapter. The row also omits that tolvaptan must be started in hospital with close sodium monitoring (FDA boxed warning).
- **[major]** `handbook/00-shared/01-chemo-basics.md`：The dexrazoxane extravasation regimen is written as a ready-to-use BSA dose, but it leaves out two label dosing limits. The daily maximum is 2,000 mg on D1 and D2 and 1,000 mg on D3, which equals a 2.0 m² BSA cap. The dose is cut by 50% when CrCl is below 40 mL/min. A large patient, or one with renal impairment (common after chemo), would get more than the labelled dose, and dexrazoxane is myelosuppressive. The same-hour (±3 h) timing of D2 and D3 is also missing.
- **[major]** `handbook/00-shared/04-irae.md`：The handbook says the severe irAEs Schoenfeld et al. (2019) saw after PD-(L)1 then osimertinib were mainly pneumonitis, hepatitis and skin reactions. The paper reports no skin reactions. The 6 severe irAEs were 4 pneumonitis, 1 colitis and 1 hepatitis. Colitis is left out and skin reactions are added. Learners will remember this list as the toxicity pattern of this drug sequence.

The 15-year figure is the USPSTF/Taiwan screening eligibility window, not when risk starts to fall. A trainee who repeats this to patients could undermine smoking-cessation counselling. It also mixes up 'risk falls' with 'risk returns to never-smoker level', which never fully happens.

- **[major]** `handbook/01-lung-cancer.md`：This row is out of date and conflicts with section 4.5 of the same chapter. It gives chemo-immunotherapy as the only first-line option and zongertinib only as second line. In fact the FDA gave zongertinib accelerated first-line approval on 2026-02-26 and sevabertinib on 2026-09-09, both for HER2 tyrosine-kinase-domain (TKD) mutations. - **[major]** `handbook/01-lung-cancer.md`：The table lists lurbinectedin monotherapy as a standard option for relapsed SCLC and supports it only with the 2020 single-arm phase 2 response rate. That is out of date. The confirmatory phase 3 LAGOON trial (topline results 2026-06-12) found no overall-survival benefit: lurbinectedin alone did numerically worse than topotecan or irinotecan. The US FDA then removed the second-line accelerated-approval indication on 2026-09-30. A learner reading this row would treat lurbinectedin as an equivalent standard option, backed by an ORR that the confirmatory trial did not turn into a survival benefit.
- **[major]** `handbook/01-lung-cancer.md`：This row defines "sensitive" relapse as ≥6 months and routes those patients to platinum rechallenge only. Two problems. (a) In SCLC trials and in ASCO guidance, the usual definition of sensitive relapse is relapse ≥90 days after first-line platinum. The 6-month figure was an older NCCN threshold for reusing the original regimen, not the definition of "sensitive". (b) NCCN SCLC v2.2026 (updated 2025-09-16) removed the chemotherapy-free-interval (CTFI) split altogether. It deleted the qualifier and footnotes recommending platinum rechallenge for CTFI >6 months, and tarlatamab is now the category 1 preferred subsequent therapy whatever the CTFI. As written, learners will memorize a wrong cut-off, and patients relapsing after 6 months would not be considered for tarlatamab.
- **[major]** `handbook/01-lung-cancer.md`：This has the same definitional error as the 'sensitive' row. In SCLC, 'resistant' relapse conventionally means relapse <90 days after first-line platinum, and 'refractory' means progression during first-line therapy. Relapse at 3-6 months is sensitive relapse under the trial and ASCO definitions. Labelling all relapses under 6 months as 抗藥型 teaches the wrong threshold, and exams commonly test this definition. Fix this together with the ≥6-month row.

As written, the row overstates when to reduce the dose, which matters most when the goal is cure and dose intensity counts. Section 7.3 of the handbook itself makes that point.

For low-risk patients, IDSA says routine empirical antifungal therapy is not recommended. - **[minor]** `handbook/00-shared/02-oncologic-emergencies.md`："尤其小細胞肺癌" suggests that SCLC is the most common lung-cancer cause of malignant SVC syndrome. SCLC's higher per-patient rate, from its central mediastinal location, is a separate fact. A learner who memorizes this line may answer "小細胞肺癌" to "SVC 症候群最常見的病因".

About half of patients recover well, about a quarter die, and the rest are left with deficits. The prognosis is serious, but a learner would memorize and could be tested on 'mostly irreversible', which is an outdated fact. It is also stated as if Sterns 2015 says it.
- **[minor]** `handbook/00-shared/02-oncologic-emergencies.md`：The text says to give dexamethasone as soon as MSCC is suspected, with no condition attached. The WARNING box just above tells readers to treat any new back pain in a cancer patient as MSCC. Read together, they would give dexamethasone to every cancer patient with back pain alone. With pain only, steroids are not routine; they are considered only for severe pain or a haematological malignancy. The missing condition exposes pain-only patients to unneeded steroid toxicity such as hyperglycaemia, GI bleeding and psychosis.

The text mixes the two up, and the split between bound and ionized calcium is a commonly tested number.

A learner could read 12–24 hours as steady state and titrate at 24 hours, which risks accumulation.
